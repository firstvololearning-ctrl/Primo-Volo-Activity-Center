Primo Volo Italian Learning Hub is a modular curriculum support system for novice Italian learners. It brings together structured language practice, printable classroom materials, cultural resources, and optional online practice to help students move from initial understanding toward increasingly independent communication.

Primo Volo is designed to help novice learners move from understanding new language to using it more independently. Students first meet vocabulary and phrases through clear visuals, audio, and modeled language. They then practice using familiar sentence patterns, retrieve the language with gradually less support, and apply it in speaking, writing, and interaction with others. Grammatical patterns are introduced through the language students are already learning and using, and the materials highlight opportunities for teachers to help students notice, compare, and explicitly explore those patterns in context as appropriate. Cultural materials connect that language to meaningful themes and contexts.

Within optional online practice, learners can follow a Practice Path within each topic and an Italy Journey across topics. These tools make completed practice visible and motivating while remaining separate from scored performance.

Why Primo Volo?

The instructional design moves learners from supported recognition toward increasingly generative, independent language use. Practice is structured to cover the full target set, gradually reduce support, and give teachers useful evidence about what may help next.

🎯 Complete, purposeful practice

Online activities vary the order of practice while ensuring eligible instructional targets are presented before a round is complete. Students get variety without important targets disappearing through random selection.

Round completion represents instructional coverage, not mastery.

🧭 Suggested Next Practice

Saved practice and scored response patterns can point toward a useful next step—such as returning to a more-supported activity, broadening practice, or trying a later activity when the available evidence supports it.

Suggestions guide practice; they are not proficiency or mastery decisions.

💬 Vocabulary becomes usable language

New vocabulary is repeatedly connected to reusable phrases and sentence structures. Students move from understanding and supported practice toward retrieval, sentence building, speaking, writing, and interaction with increasing independence.

Reusable carrier phrases

Familiar sentence frames give novice learners a stable structure while the vocabulary changes. Students can begin using new words in meaningful language instead of practicing only isolated vocabulary.

Io vedo… Io ho… Mi piace… Non mi piace… È… C’è…
🔎

Grammar in context

The language students are already using creates natural opportunities to draw attention to Italian structure.

👂 Model it repeatedly
💡 Notice it aloud
🔍 Highlight or compare it
✏️ Teach it explicitly

This may include articles and noun patterns, gender and number, agreement, recurring verb forms, word order, and question-and-answer structures.

👥

From practice to interaction

Many resources move beyond individual practice. Partner and small-group games give students repeated opportunities to ask, listen, answer, choose, describe, and express preferences using familiar language.

Chat Mats provide visual vocabulary and reusable sentence frames for supported partner talk. Teachers can model a question and response, use the mat as needed during practice, and gradually reduce support as students become more comfortable using the language independently.

The same target vocabulary can therefore be practiced through recognition, sentence building, retrieval, and real conversational turns.

Starting Checks

Starting Checks are being added topic by topic to give teachers a clearer baseline before instruction or practice begins.

When available, a check uses distinct task types for written/visual vocabulary recognition, listening recognition, and recognition of carrier phrases relevant to the topic. Vocabulary and carrier-phrase results are kept separate so different kinds of knowledge are not collapsed into one score.

Starting Checks are intended to help identify a useful instructional starting point. They are not grades, mastery decisions, readiness measures, or ACTFL proficiency ratings.

🗺️

Practice Path & Italy Journey

Within optional online practice, the Practice Path shows which activities have been practiced within a topic. As learners explore topics, the Italy Journey turns that completed practice into a ten-city route. Every two explored topics unlock a new city and a short discovery connected to the associated region.

These are completion and motivation tools, not mastery scores.

📊

Progress & Suggested Next Practice

Progress records responses in scored online activities and summarizes accuracy by activity, topic, and target word or expression. This keeps performance evidence separate from the completion-based Practice Path and Italy Journey.

Suggested Next Practice combines scored response patterns with Practice Path completion to offer an actionable next step. After enough scored responses are available, higher accuracy may point toward another available activity, while lower accuracy may suggest revisiting a more-supported activity. Suggestions also account for activities the learner has already practiced and can be selected to open the recommended topic and activity directly.

These recommendations guide practice; they are not mastery, proficiency, or readiness decisions. None of these displays is an ACTFL proficiency rating or a formal mastery assessment.

Explicit grammar support continues to expand, always connected to meaningful language and context. The current materials already let students hear, see, retrieve, and use recurring structures so that teachers can choose when to model them, point them out, or teach them more explicitly. The online activities remain optional; the printable resources are designed to be useful on their own.
